# Observation and Coaching


### What to observe SM & How to coach teams

While the Scrum Master ensures the DSU happens and stays within the 15-minute timebox, the event belongs to the Developers. 
SM shouldn't be the dedicated note-taker at the Daily Standup (DSU).If the Scrum Master takes all the notes, updates Jira/ClickUp tickets live, and moves cards around during the meeting, it subtly creates a dynamic where developers report to the Scrum Master rather than talking to each other.
 
  * **Why Developers Should Own Notes and Board Updates:**
 - [ ] Self-Organization: Teams build accountability when they own their workflow tools and updates.
 - [ ] Active Engagement: Updating cards or taking quick notes keeps team members actively engaged rather than passively listening to a status report.
 - [ ] Servant Leadership: My role as a Scrum Master is to observe team dynamics, identify hidden dependencies or blockers, and facilitate — not to act as an administrative secretary.

* **How to Ask and Shift Note-Taking to the Team:**
Rather than simply stopping cold turkey, guide the team into owning the process using these practical coaching approaches:

1. Frame it as a Self-Organization Challenge (The "Why")

Bring it up during a Retrospective or at the end of a DSU:
"Team, the Daily Standup is your space to synchronize on our progress toward the Sprint Goal and spot blockers.
To help us self-organize better and keep everyone engaged, I'd like us to take turns running the board and taking notes on action items or blockers."

2. Rotate the "Board Pilot" / Note-Taker
Introduce a rotating role so one person isn't stuck doing it every day. You can call it the "Captain of the Day," "Flight Controller," or "Jira Jockey."

- [ ]How it works: Alphabetically or by turn, a developer shares their screen, navigates the board, and writes down key action items/blockers during that DSU.
- [ ]Pro-tip: The person who was "Captain" today passes the baton to the next person for tomorrow.

3. Use Automated & Visual Blocker Capture
Encourage the team to capture notes directly where the work lives instead of creating separate meeting minutes:

- [ ]In Jira/ClickUp: Ask developers to flag tickets as Blocked or add a quick comment directly on the card during or immediately after the DSU.
- [ ]In Slack/Teams: Set up a quick automation or channel (e.g., #sprint-blockers) where the developer posting the impediment writes a one-line summary.

4. Step Back Gently (The Coaching Technique)

- [ ]Pass the mic: Instead of sharing your screen, ask a developer:
"Who would like to share their screen today?"

- [ ]Ask, don't write: If a blocker comes up, ask: 
"Great callout—who is going to pop that blocker tag on the ticket?" or "Can someone drop that action item in our Slack channel?"

What the Scrum Master Should Write Down During DSU

While you aren't taking general meeting minutes, I, as a Scrum master should listen actively and jot down your my personal follow-ups:

- [ ]Systemic Blockers: Organizational friction, external team dependencies, or environment outages that you need to help clear after the call.
- [ ]Behavioral Patterns: Is someone constantly silent? Are scope items creeping in unannounced?
- [ ]Offline Sync Needs: Write down who needs to talk after the DSU (e.g., "Alex and Sam need a 5-minute breakout on the API schema").

This can be automated. While Jira natively does not have an AI listener built directly into live audio, I can integrate AI Meeting Assistants / Agents directly into our virtual meeting platform (Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet) and connect them to Jira via webhooks, automation, or native app integrations.
